Digital Twins & Model-Informed Controls

A digital twin is more than a dashboard or standalone simulation. It connects an executable model to the state of a physical or representative system so engineers can compare behavior, test scenarios, anticipate faults, and improve decisions.

01 · Physical systemSensors, actuators, equipment, and environmental context.
02 · Live stateEPICS PVs, alarms, histories, configuration, and inferred state.
03 · Executable modelPhysics, controls logic, data-driven models, or a hybrid of all three.
04 · Decision loopScenario testing, anomaly detection, prediction, and governed feedback.
IMPLEMENTED SIMULATION

Adaptive Accelerator Timing Twin

A distributed EPICS simulation models the environment, oscillator, laser, and feedback controller as separate communicating processes. It allows adaptive PID strategies to be exercised through realistic PV interfaces and observed under drift and disturbance scenarios. Because it represents a generalized accelerator timing loop rather than maintaining synchronization with one production asset, I describe it precisely as a digital-twin prototype.

TWIN-READY FOUNDATION

Precision Motion & Geometric Correction

The motion-correction system already maintains a software representation of stage geometry and converts between physical and corrected coordinates. Adding synchronized telemetry, uncertainty estimates, calibration history, and replayable command scenarios would turn that model-based controls layer into a true operational twin.

CONCEPTUAL DESIGN

Lunar Waste-to-Resource (W2R) System

This independent concept proposes a modular system for plasma pyrolysis, electrolysis-based resource recovery, additive manufacturing, and safe residual-waste handling in a lunar habitat. Its EPICS-based supervisory layer and Python digital twin would estimate material and energy flows, compare observed performance with predicted behavior, explore failures, and support predictive maintenance and power-aware scheduling.

Project status and provenance: Developed as a conceptual response to NASA's LunaRecycle Challenge and aligned with Artemis sustainability goals. It has not been built, awarded, commissioned, or endorsed by NASA. Claims in the proposal are design targets and engineering estimates requiring prototype validation.
